FlyMeThrough: Human-AI Collaborative 3D Indoor Mapping with Commodity Drones

What is it about?

*FlyMeThrough* is a human-AI collaborative system designed to make 3D indoor mapping accessible using standard, off-the-shelf consumer drones. The system addresses the high costs and complexity associated with professional indoor scanning equipment. It uses a three-stage pipeline: (1) **Structure from Motion** to turn drone footage into a 3D model, (2) **User Annotation** to mark important indoor objects (POIs) directly on video frames, and (3) **Depth-guided Raycasting** to project those 2D annotations into 3D space, creating an object-infused 3D map.

Key Innovation

The primary novelty is the **human-in-the-loop approach to semantic mapping**. Rather than relying on autonomous object detection (which struggles with "open-vocabulary" items like specific AED kits or emergency exits), the system leverages the **local, contextual knowledge of non-experts**. By allowing users to point out objects of interest in 2D video, the AI performs the complex technical heavy lifting (segmentation, raycasting, and clustering) to place those objects accurately within the 3D model.

Potential Applications

  • **Building Management:** Asset management, event planning, and remote facility inspection.
  • **Navigation & Accessibility:** Helping users map out paths for individuals with mobility needs or identifying key navigation landmarks.
  • **Safety:** Cataloging emergency equipment (e.g., fire extinguishers, AEDs) and mapping escape routes.
  • **Community Mapping:** Empowering non-professionals to create digital twins of public indoor spaces without needing specialized CAD or 3D modeling skills.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Device-Agnostic Design:** The system works with any consumer drone capable of recording 2.7K video, democratizing the mapping process.
  • **Social Acceptance:** Users were found to be surprisingly comfortable with drones flying near them, provided the flights were managed appropriately.
  • **Privacy-First Approach:** The authors suggest three core guidelines to address privacy concerns: spatial exclusion (avoiding sensitive areas), temporal minimization (flying during off-hours), and automated face-filtering in the footage.
  • **Efficiency:** The system is remarkably fast, requiring under 10 minutes of flight time per space and generating maps in semi-real time (up to 30 minutes).

    • Limitations

  • **Dependency on Proprietary Tools:** The current pipeline relies on external photogrammetry software (Metashape) for the initial 3D reconstruction, which may limit full open-source independence.
  • **Reconstruction Failures:** In the study, 1 out of 12 spaces failed to reconstruct correctly, suggesting that certain complex environments may still pose challenges for standard consumer drone optics.
  • **Annotation Limitations:** Approximately 9% of user annotations failed to process into valid 3D bounding boxes, suggesting the system is not yet 100% robust in translating 2D inputs to 3D space.
  • **Depth Estimation Sensitivity:** Because the system uses depth-guided raycasting, the accuracy of object placement in the 3D map is inherently dependent on the quality of the metric depth estimation.

    What is it about?

    "Morphing Skin" is a soft, skin-like platform designed to embed multimodal fluidic actuation into interactive surfaces. It utilizes flexible **electro-osmotic pumps (EOPs)**—devices that generate hydraulic flow through electrochemical reactions when a low voltage is applied—to move fluid within a stretchable, layered structure. By pumping fluid between reservoirs and output actuators, the system can dynamically change the device's shape, force, weight distribution, and optical properties.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation lies in the use of flexible, thin-film electro-osmotic pumps as a compact alternative to traditional, bulky mechanical pumps and valves. By integrating these pumps directly into a stretchable silicone substrate using flexible printed circuits, the system achieves a high degree of form-factor freedom while remaining thin, lightweight, and capable of complex multimodal outputs (e.g., suction/extrusion forces and visual/refractive changes).

    Potential Applications

  • **Soft Robotics:** Creating adaptive robotic grippers that can sense objects via suction or pressure, and emotional companion robots that use surface deformation to convey "feelings."
  • **Wearables:** A smart wrist brace that provides haptic guidance (pressing force) and visual data visualization for fitness or health tracking.
  • **Interactive Media:** "Magic" pages in pop-up books or dynamic lampshades that use fluid-driven shape changes and light refraction to create immersive, animated displays.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Multimodality:** The system does not just perform one action; it simultaneously handles force (extrusion/suction), shape change (hinges/kirigami), optics (dynamic lenses), and center-of-gravity manipulation.
  • **Emotional Expression:** The researchers demonstrated that simple hydraulic surface deformations could make a desktop robot appear "excited" or "frightened," showcasing the potential for non-verbal robot-human communication.
  • **Fabrication Method:** The system is built using a multi-layer assembly process that involves heat-pressing membranes and electrodes, coating them with silicone, and manual fluid injection, demonstrating a accessible path for soft-interface fabrication.
  • **Voltage Efficiency:** By using EOP technology, the system operates at lower voltages (well below 1 kV) compared to other high-voltage electro-hydraulic alternatives.

    • Limitations

  • **Degradation:** Long-term durability is a concern; the researchers noted a decrease in performance over time due to gas generation (a byproduct of the electrochemical process) and potential bonding degradation.
  • **Asymmetric Performance:** The pumps currently exhibit asymmetric pressure output when fluid is moved between different sides.
  • **Non-linearity:** The actuators exhibit non-linear performance under constant voltage, making precise control more challenging.
  • **Current Form Factor:** While flexible, the platform is currently limited to planar configurations; achieving complex 3D shapes requires folding or origami-inspired techniques.

    What is it about?

    **ShoeGenAI** is a creativity support tool designed to bridge the gap between abstract design intent and manufacturing feasibility in the footwear industry. It addresses the struggles designers face with repetitive tasks, design fixation, and the lack of domain-specific knowledge in general-purpose AI tools. The system consists of three core modules: a **Generator** (fine-tuned with shoe-specific data to create feasible designs), a **Combiner** (utilizing SAM and Stable Diffusion to merge shoe parts like soles and uppers), and an **Editor** (using a Pix2Pix model for precise partial modifications like color or material changes).

    Key Innovation

    The innovation lies in its **domain-specific modularity**. Unlike generic image generators (like DALL-E 3 or Midjourney), ShoeGenAI integrates technical footwear domain knowledge into its latent space, ensuring that generated output adheres to structural and manufacturing constraints. By separating the process into generation, part-based combination, and controlled editing, the system provides designers with the precise, incremental control necessary for professional production workflows.

    Potential Applications

  • **Professional Shoe Designers:** To rapidly prototype concepts, overcome design fixation, and improve communication with manufacturers.
  • **Novice Designers:** To explore diverse creative possibilities and overcome difficulties in crafting complex text prompts.
  • **Cross-Domain Adaptation:** The modular architecture is designed to be transferable to other fashion or industrial design fields (e.g., hats, apparel) requiring structural feasibility.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Divergent User Needs:** Professionals prioritized "feasibility" and speed, while novices valued the tool for creative exploration and breaking out of repetitive design patterns.
  • **Addressing "Design Fixation":** The tool helps users move beyond their habitual patterns by offering template-based generation and easy part combinations.
  • **Partial Editing:** The system allows for adjustments (like changing a material or color) without the "burdensome" process of regenerating an entire image from scratch, preserving the original design intent.
  • **Templated Prompting:** Recognizing that not all designers are comfortable with "prompt engineering," the system offers a template-based UI to democratize access to high-quality AI generation.

    • Limitations

  • **Limited Scope:** Currently, the training dataset is primarily focused on sneakers, limiting its utility for other footwear categories like high heels.
  • **Time Efficiency for Novices:** While novices found the tool creative, the transcript notes they did not necessarily perceive it as a time-saver compared to the faster, more goal-oriented experience of professionals.
  • **Real-World Verification:** While the research demonstrates high manufacturability, the system has not yet fully integrated the entire production pipeline (e.g., automated pattern generation or final physical prototyping), which remains future work.

    **MEDebiaser** (referred to in the transcript as *Medvisor*) is a human-AI interactive system designed to mitigate biases in multi-label medical image classification. It addresses the challenges of imbalanced datasets and the inefficient, high-communication feedback loops between physicians and machine learning engineers. The system allows physicians to visualize model attention (via Grad-CAM), identify problematic images where the model is failing, and provide targeted feedback by drawing polygons over correct clinical regions. This feedback is then used to fine-tune the model through a joint loss function that balances prediction accuracy with attention alignment.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is the shift in the collaboration dynamic: instead of physicians performing exhaustive, manual data labeling (the traditional bottleneck), the system uses an **iterative, human-in-the-loop attention-correction mechanism**. By allowing physicians to interact directly with the model's "attention" rather than just the dataset labels, the system embeds medical expertise directly into the model training process without requiring the physician to have deep machine learning knowledge.

    Potential Applications

    This system is designed for use by **medical professionals and AI researchers/engineers** working in clinical settings. It is particularly useful for medical image analysis where rare diseases are underrepresented or where multiple conditions often co-occur in the same scan (e.g., chest X-rays), as it allows for rapid model improvement on complex, "long-tail" clinical cases.

    Interesting Points

  • **Targeted Intervention:** Physicians no longer need to annotate the entire dataset; they can focus exclusively on problematic cases identified by the system (e.g., low accuracy or scattered attention), significantly reducing workload.
  • **Transparent Reasoning:** The use of Grad-CAM heat maps allows physicians to see *why* a model is making a decision, which helps build trust and validates whether the model is looking at the correct medical evidence.
  • **Role Rebalancing:** The system effectively shifts the physician from a data-labeling role to an active model-supervision role, while engineers move into a more technical, supporting capacity.
  • **Visualizing Complexity:** The system provides dashboards (tables and chord diagrams) to help users intuitively understand label distributions and co-occurrence patterns within their datasets.

    • Limitations

  • **Metric Alignment:** The current recommendation strategy for identifying problematic images is based on model-centric metrics rather than clinical or medical-centric metrics.

    What is it about?

    The research introduces the **"Privacy Auditing Sandbox,"** a tool designed to help end users investigate how their personal data influences online recommendation systems and targeted advertising. Instead of relying on passive observation or risky, real-world data sharing, users create synthetic "personas" with specific attributes (e.g., age, income). The system replaces the user's actual browser data with these synthetic profiles, interacts with websites to capture ads, and uses a Large Language Model (LLM) to score and visualize how specific attributes drive the recommendations they receive.

    Key Innovation

    The primary novelty lies in **controlled persona-based auditing.** By enabling users to systematically isolate and vary a single attribute (like income) while keeping other demographic and longitudinal factors constant, the system provides a "causal" look at how algorithms respond to specific inputs. The use of an automated pipeline—replacing browser data, detecting ads, and using LLMs to quantify alignment scores—transforms vague user suspicion into objective, comparable evidence.

    Potential Applications

  • **Empowering End Users:** Allowing privacy-conscious individuals to audit websites in real-time to see how they are being profiled.
  • **Digital Literacy & Education:** Helping users understand the link between their personal data and the "black box" of recommendation engines.
  • **Regulatory Support:** Providing a methodology for researchers or advocates to gather evidence of discriminatory or opaque targeting practices by platforms.

    • Interesting Points

  • **From Discomfort to Informed Concern:** Engagement with the sandbox moved users from "vague discomfort" to "informed concern," providing them with the concrete evidence needed to understand platform overreach.
  • **Visualizing Hidden Profiles:** The system creates a tangible map (a distribution plot) where users can clearly see if a "high-income" persona triggers different ad clusters compared to a "low-income" one.
  • **Synthetic Data Replacement:** The system doesn't just block ads; it acts as a "data proxy," tricking trackers into thinking the user is someone else to reveal what information the platform is actually prioritizing.
  • **Ambivalence:** The study revealed that even when users were empowered by the tool, they remained conflicted—they appreciated the relevance of personalized content while simultaneously feeling uneasy about the depth of inferences being made.

    • Limitations

  • **Scope of Simulation:** As noted during the Q&A, the current system focuses primarily on demographic and historical data; it does not yet account for complex behavioral signals like mouse movements, dwell time, or social relationships.
  • **Platform Complexity:** Many modern recommendation systems rely on non-static, multi-dimensional data points that may be difficult to fully replicate with a synthetic persona, potentially limiting the system’s effectiveness against more sophisticated, real-time algorithms.
  • **Technical Overhead:** While the tool is designed for end users, it requires a level of setup and technical engagement that may still be inaccessible to the average, non-technical consumer.

    What is it about?

    VIP-Sim is an open-source, user-centered vision impairment simulation tool designed to help designers create more accessible interfaces. Unlike traditional simulators that focus on single impairments, VIP-Sim provides 21 adjustable symptom shaders (e.g., photosensitivity, field loss, blur) that can be combined to simulate complex, real-world visual conditions. It functions as a desktop overlay, allowing designers to test accessibility in any software (such as Figma or Adobe XD) without needing specialized hardware like VR headsets.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is the **"Mono Witness Protocol,"** a participatory design process where people with varying eyesight and specific vision impairments helped calibrate the simulation shaders in real-time. By comparing the simulator's output to their own vision, participants ensured the shaders accurately reflected their lived experiences. Additionally, the system uses webcam-based eye tracking to dynamically adjust simulations, providing a seamless workflow that does not interrupt the designer's creative process.

    Potential Applications

  • **Accessible UI/UX Design:** Allowing designers to identify accessibility barriers (e.g., poor contrast, small text) early in the development cycle across various platforms, including 3D modeling software.
  • **Empathy Building:** Educating design teams on the challenges faced by users with specific vision impairments.
  • **Going Beyond Guidelines:** Supplementing rigid standards (like WCAG) with visual simulations that account for nuanced, compounding symptoms that standard checklists often miss.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Participatory Calibration:** Each of the 21 shaders was validated by participants with actual visual impairments to ensure realistic representation, avoiding stereotypical or inaccurate simulations.
  • **Workflow Integration:** By operating as a software overlay rather than requiring specialized VR/AR hardware, it removes technical barriers to adoption for professional designers.
  • **Layering Symptoms:** The tool allows for the simulation of multiple concurrent symptoms (e.g., combining hyperopia with color vision deficiency), reflecting the reality that many users experience multiple accompanying impairments.
  • **Ethical Guardrails:** The researchers explicitly emphasize that the tool is intended to aid design, not replace direct user testing with individuals who have visual impairments.

    • Limitations

  • **Not a Replacement for Users:** The tool cannot fully replicate the human visual system or the subjective experience of living with an impairment; it remains a simulation that requires final verification by the target user group.
  • **Risk of Misuse:** The researchers acknowledge that simulators can potentially reinforce ableism or lead to "oversimplification" if designers use them as a substitute for inclusive, participatory design practices.
  • **Infinite Combinations:** Because the 21 symptoms are adjustable and combinable, the sheer number of possible permutations is massive, making it difficult to guarantee "blanket" accessibility through simulation alone.

    What is it about?

    The research addresses the challenge of designing user interface (UI) layouts in mixed reality (MR), where the number of possible configurations is nearly infinite. The authors propose a "preference-guided multi-objective UI adaptation" system that treats layout design as a multi-objective optimization problem. Instead of using static weights to solve this, the system observes a user’s manual UI manipulations to infer their implicit preferences (e.g., favoring visibility over symmetry) and dynamically adjusts the optimization process to focus on a "Pareto frontier"—the set of best possible trade-offs—that aligns with those preferences.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is the use of **online human preference inference** to guide a **lexicographic multi-objective optimization**. Rather than asking users to explicitly define weights for various UI objectives, the system passively tracks how a user interacts with elements to compute preference ranks. It then uses these ranks to focus the search for optimal layouts on the parts of the Pareto frontier most relevant to the user's immediate goals.

    Potential Applications

    This system is designed for **Mixed Reality (MR) interfaces**, where users often need to arrange multiple widgets or panels in a 3D space. It could be useful for:
  • **Personalized Workspace Productivity:** Automatically arranging tools and windows based on a user's workflow.
  • **Accessibility:** Helping users with limited range of motion by prioritizing reachability or visibility based on their interactions.
  • **Adaptive Dashboards:** Reducing manual fatigue by "auto-completing" layout adjustments as the user moves elements.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Dynamic Adaptation:** The system recalculates its focus in real-time as user preferences shift, allowing the interface to evolve naturally during a task.
  • **Lexicographical Prioritization:** By categorizing objectives into high, mid, and low priority, the system creates a "focused" Pareto frontier, ensuring the most important constraints (as perceived by the user) are satisfied first.
  • **Efficiency Gains:** The study results showed that the system required fewer manual user movements to reach a desirable layout compared to standard optimization methods.
  • **Implicit Goal Capture:** The system assumes that every user movement is purposeful; for example, moving a music player closer to a phone is treated as an intentional signal that "semantic alignment" or "reachability" should be prioritized.

    • Limitations

  • **Predefined Objectives:** The current implementation relies on a set of pre-crafted objective functions defined by experts (e.g., visibility, alignment), rather than learning entirely new objectives from scratch.
  • **Naive Preference Inference:** The method for tracking user preferences currently uses a simple moving average filter, which may not capture the full complexity or uncertainty of human intent.
  • **Optimization Complexity:** The approach requires a mathematical framework that is computationally non-trivial, and the authors note that more advanced methods (e.g., Bayesian inference or trust-region optimization) may be needed to handle the "noise" of human behavior more effectively.

    What is it about?

    **NeuroSync** is a system designed to address the "misalignment" problem in coding-based human-AI interaction. The researchers found that users often struggle with large language models (LLMs) because of "bidirectional ambiguity"—the loss of information that occurs when a user converts nonlinear intentions into linear text prompts, and the LLM attempts to reconstruct those intentions into code. NeuroSync replaces the standard "text-in, text-out" chat interface with a graph-based representation of the LLM’s "understanding." Instead of just prompting, users can visualize the model’s internal task graph, directly modify the nodes and structure to correct the model's logic, and then pass that updated graph back to the LLM to generate more accurate code.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is **direct LLM understanding modification**. By externalizing the LLM's internal task representation as a graph, the system transforms the interaction from an indirect conversation into a direct collaboration. The researchers also developed a **knowledge distillation pipeline** that trains smaller models to generate these complex graphs, allowing for low-latency, real-time performance (roughly 40x faster than standard LLM processing) without sacrificing significant accuracy.

    Potential Applications

  • **Software Engineering:** Professional developers using AI assistants to build complex, multi-step applications (e.g., data scrapers that save images and text in specific sequences).
  • **Complex Task Automation:** Any domain where a user needs to guide an LLM through a non-linear, multi-stage process that usually requires constant "prompt engineering" and back-and-forth correction.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Bidirectional Ambiguity:** The researchers identified that the root cause of AI failure in coding isn't just the model's inability to code, but the translation loss between the user’s non-linear intent and the AI’s linear response.
  • **Cognitive Load Management:** They implemented "intent-aware abstraction" and a recursive graph simplification algorithm to ensure that the visualization doesn't overwhelm the user.
  • **Process Compression:** By editing the underlying logic graph rather than iteratively chatting, a task that might take seven steps in a traditional chatbot interface can often be resolved in two.
  • **Multi-Agent Training:** The team used a multi-agent system to generate synthetic datasets to train the distilled, smaller model, proving that advanced logical mapping doesn't always require a massive, slow LLM.

    • Limitations

  • **Visual/Graph Complexity:** While the system uses graph simplification, there remains a potential challenge regarding visual scalability; as tasks grow significantly in length or complexity, managing a large graph may still become mentally taxing for the user.
  • **Distillation Trade-off:** While the smaller distilled model is significantly faster, the researchers noted a small drop in accuracy (retaining 90% accuracy compared to the larger model).
  • **Dependency on System Logic:** The system relies on the model’s ability to generate an accurate initial "understanding graph." If the model fails to represent the task logic correctly in the first place, the user must perform more heavy-duty corrections to the graph.

  • **Innate Behaviors:** Micro-movements (e.g., twitching) and scripted motion sequences.

    • Potential Applications

    This system is useful for VR designers, researchers in human-computer interaction (HCI), and developers building immersive simulations, training environments, or entertainment applications.     **SoilSense** is a tangible interface that utilizes soil-based microbial fuel cells (SMFCs) as a sensing medium.     Key Innovation

    The core novelty lies in **repurposing a biological energy-harvesting device into a tactile sensor.** By observing that mechanical pressure reduces gaps and dry spots within the soil-fuel cell structure—thereby improving ionic transportation—the researchers transformed ubiquitous, biocompatible soil into a low-cost, sustainable, and organic input material for human-computer interaction.

    Potential Applications

  • **Accessible/DIY Interfaces:** Due to the low cost and availability of materials, it is ideal for rapid, sustainable prototyping of tangible devices.
  • **Human-Plant Interaction:** Embedding sensors into soil-based plant pots to create novel input channels linked to the plant's environment.
  • **Surface and Weight Sensing:** By scaling up, the technology can detect weight distribution or pressure across a large surface (e.g., smart flooring or large-scale installations).
  • **Plastic/Organic Design:** The system can be molded into custom shapes (e.g., animal figures) to leverage the natural affordances of the soil.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Material Affordance:** Soil is treated as a design material that is both plastic (malleable) and functional (sensing), allowing for unique form factors.

  • **Interaction vs. Autonomy:** The researchers explored a design trade-off between "fully self-powered" (low interactivity) and "plug-in" modes (high interactivity), suggesting varying degrees of energy autonomy depending on the use case.
  • **Fabrication:** The sensors are simple enough to be created from local soil, highlighting the project's focus on accessible, sustainable fabrication.

    What is it about?

    UltraEdit is an in-situ (within the VR environment) authoring tool designed to simplify the creation of ultrasound mid-air haptic patterns. Unlike traditional desktop-based tools that require users to switch between environments, UltraEdit allows designers to manipulate haptic properties directly inside a VR workspace. It uses "blobs"—a tangible, visual metaphor—to represent haptic sensations. Users interact with these blobs using bare-hand gestures (stretching, squishing, pulling) to adjust parameters like intensity and frequency without breaking immersion.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is the **"blob-based" design paradigm**. By representing abstract haptic data (frequency/intensity) as deformable 3D objects, the system translates complex technical parameters into an intuitive, clay-like manipulation experience. This shifts the design process from numerical input to physical interaction, effectively "democratizing" haptic design for users without specialized technical expertise.

    Potential Applications

  • **Immersive VR Development:** Allowing VR creators to design tactile feedback for virtual objects in real-time while experiencing the simulation.
  • **Education and Art:** Providing a tool for non-experts (artists, students, or designers) to integrate haptics into their projects without needing deep knowledge of haptic rendering parameters.
  • **Prototyping:** Rapid iteration of haptic sensations for various hardware configurations.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Intuitive Editing:** Users can map frequency and intensity to specific physical movements—stretching the blob horizontally changes frequency, while vertical stretching adjusts intensity.
  • **Temporal and Spatial Flexibility:** The system supports the creation of complex patterns through "time blobs" (for temporal changes) and a unique hand-tracing method, where users can draw haptic paths on their own palms to transfer shapes to virtual objects.
  • **Seamless Workflow:** The use of "tethered lines" that can be physically "cut" with a hand gesture provides a natural and satisfying way to disconnect or assign haptic sensations.
  • **User Satisfaction:** Study results showed that participants preferred UltraEdit over traditional desktop tools regarding novelty, attractiveness, and efficiency, despite achieving similar completion times.

    • Limitations

  • **Precision Constraints:** Because the system relies on gestural stretching for values, it can be difficult to achieve exact numerical settings compared to traditional sliders or keypad inputs (though the developers suggest sensitivity tuning as a partial solution).
  • **Compound Editing:** The current iteration requires users to "split" compound blobs into individual parts to copy or edit them, as there is currently no direct way to manipulate a group of blobs as a single new object.
  • **Handedness/Customization:** While the system supports both hands for interactions, complex pattern management is still evolving, and the tool is currently optimized specifically for the ultrasound hardware workflow, requiring adaptation for other haptic feedback devices.

    What is it about?

    Sculpin is a programming-by-demonstration system designed to bring the direct-manipulation principles found in spreadsheets or graphic editors to the domain of JSON data. Instead of writing code to transform data or build interfaces, users interact directly with the JSON document. They select parts of the data, apply transformations, and the system records these actions into a reusable, reactive program.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is the "sculpting" interaction model, which relies on three main components:
  • **Local transformations:** Users perform small, local edits that are recorded in a timeline.
  • **Scratch spaces:** A mechanism to attach key-value pairs to parts of the medium, providing necessary "room" for storing intermediate data or state during complex tasks.
  • **Generalized selections:** The ability to select data and then refine or "generalize" those selections (e.g., applying an action to all items of a certain type) to perform programmatic tasks without writing explicit loops or control flow.

    • Potential Applications

  • **Data Wrangling:** Transforming data formats (e.g., converting JSON to GeoJSON for mapping) without manual coding.
  • **Rapid UI Prototyping:** Turning raw data into interactive elements (images, cards, text boxes) and building reactive interfaces like to-do lists or search tools.
  • **Task Automation:** Automating repetitive data-processing workflows for both end-users and programmers.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Reactive Programs:** Because the system records actions into a timeline, dropping new input data into the system automatically triggers a rerun of the program to produce updated output.
  • **Blurring Data and UI:** Sculpin demonstrates that the same manipulation techniques can be used to reshape data structure and to build functional interface components (buttons, search bars).
  • **Extensibility:** The researchers suggest that the "sculpting" paradigm—local edits, scratch spaces, and generalized selections—is media-agnostic and could be extended to text documents, web pages, or even canvas-based pixel editing.
  • **Programming for Programmers:** While intended to lower the barrier for end-users, the presenters acknowledge that Sculpin is also a powerful tool for professional developers to perform quick, visual data wrangling that can be embedded into larger codebases.

    • Limitations

  • **Editing Challenges:** Acknowledged in the paper, it is difficult to go back and edit a specific step within a recorded demonstration sequence once it is in the middle of a program.
  • **Scope of JSON:** The presenters admit that direct JSON manipulation is a somewhat specialized task that average end-users may not frequently encounter in daily life.

    What is it about?

    **ForcePinch** is an interaction technique for Extended Reality (XR) that allows users to dynamically control the "tracking speed"—the mapping ratio between hand movement and pointer/object movement—by varying the amount of pinch force applied between their fingers. Using a friction-based metaphor, the system slows down movement (high precision) when the user pinches harder and speeds up movement (high reachability) when the user pinches lightly. The system utilizes a pressure sensor prototype and maps force data to tracking speed via a calibrated cubic spline curve.

    Key Innovation

    The core novelty lies in using **continuous, input-side force modulation** to adjust spatial navigation and object manipulation on the fly. Unlike distance-responsive or velocity-responsive methods, which rely on movement patterns, ForcePinch offloads control to a separate modality (pinch force), allowing users to decouple their physical hand movement from the resulting cursor speed for more nuanced, granular control.

    Potential Applications

  • **Precision Tasks:** Fine-tuning object placement in 3D environments or detailed 2D path tracing.
  • **Productivity:** Navigating large 1D/2D interfaces (like sliders or menus) in XR where both rapid travel and pinpoint selection are required.
  • **Creative Tools:** The research suggests future extensions such as controlling stroke thickness in 3D drawing, adjusting selection bubble sizes, or using force-based signatures/gestures.

    Graffiti is a research system designed to decentralize social media by lowering the barrier to entry for building personalized social applications. Currently, social apps are "one-size-fits-all," making it difficult for users to find spaces tailored to specific communities. Graffiti provides a generic infrastructure that handles back-end complexity (login, storage, discovery), allowing developers to build functional, interoperable social apps using only front-end code. It uses a flexible data model that allows different apps—such as microblogging, e-commerce, or community-specific tools—to read and write to the same data, ensuring users can switch platforms without losing their social connections.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is the application of a **"folksonomy"** approach to the entire social stack. Unlike rigid protocols (like ActivityPub) that embed specific design patterns into their APIs, Graffiti treats content, interactions, and discovery channels as extensible, community-driven strings. By employing **"total reification,"** where every interaction (likes, replies, edits, moderation) is stored as an independent object that points to the target content, the system avoids "baking in" specific features, allowing developers to create highly niche social behaviors that can still interoperate.

    Potential Applications

  • **Niche Community Spaces:** Building highly specialized social tools for housing co-ops, hobbyist clubs, or local neighborhood groups that don't need to scale to "billions."
  • **Diverse Application Ecosystems:** Enabling a variety of app types—from Tinder-like discovery to Wikipedia-style collaboration—to coexist and share underlying data.
  • **Customized Moderation:** Allowing users or communities to define their own moderation systems by creating "remove" or "vote" objects, which other users can choose to adopt or ignore.

  • **Design for Imperfection:** Instead of forcing global standards, Graffiti accepts that different apps may use slightly different labels for data, relying on social pressure and "fuzzy" convergence to align standards over time.
  • **Total Reification:** By turning interactions into objects, the system effectively turns every action into a data point, enabling novel features like retroactive moderation or democratic collaborative editing.
  • **Context Control via Channels:** To prevent "context collapse" (e.g., mixing dating and work profiles), Graffiti uses "channels" as a primary discovery mechanism, ensuring data only surfaces in the specific contexts intended by the developer/user.

    What is it about?

    **GenTune** is a system designed to assist environment designers in using generative AI for creative work. It addresses the "black box" nature of current AI tools, which often produce complex, opaque prompts that are difficult for designers to edit precisely. GenTune provides a workflow where designers start with a high-level concept, generate a structured prompt and image, and then use two core features—**Traceable Prompts** and **Semantic Guided Refinement**—to selectively edit specific elements of an image without sacrificing visual consistency.

    Key Innovation

    The innovation lies in **bridging the gap between visual elements and underlying text prompts**. GenTune allows users to select a region on an image, which then triggers a "trace" back to the original structured prompt. This creates a transparent mapping where a designer can see exactly which text label governs a specific visual object. By modifying these labels, the system offers semantic control over refinements, enabling edits that are more coherent and aesthetically consistent than traditional, localized inpainting.

    Potential Applications

  • **Film and TV Production:** Assisting environment designers in rapid ideation and iteration of set visuals.
  • **Client Communication:** Reducing the frequency of meetings by allowing designers to make real-time, precise refinements during client sessions.
  • **Creative Workflows:** Serving as a tool for professional artists to manage complex design specifications that require high structural and lighting consistency.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Traceable Agency:** The system allows designers to treat AI-generated images as editable objects rather than static results, solving the "trial and error" frustration common in prompt engineering.
  • **Agentic Workflows:** The authors highlight that as generative pipelines become more complex (e.g., generating scripts and storyboards before visuals), the ability for humans to intervene and control these "intermediary representations" becomes critical.
  • **Methodology:** The system uses CLIP similarity to map specific visual segments to text labels, ensuring that the interface is intuitive and context-aware.
  • **Professional Adoption:** The tool has been validated through both controlled studies and long-term deployment in a professional studio, proving its utility in actual commercial environments.

    • Limitations

    What is it about?

    KinemaFX is an interactive system designed to help non-expert users create and customize 3D particle effects. The research addresses the high artistic skill barrier and steep learning curve associated with professional particle software. The system works by decoupling particle effects into two components: **semantic** (what the object is/looks like) and **kinematic** (how it moves/behaves). Users define their intent through primitive graphical and texture inputs, then iteratively refine the results through a guided exploration workflow that balances these semantic and kinematic attributes.

    Key Innovation

    The core innovation is a **unified structure representation** that bridges the gap between user intent and complex particle behaviors. By using simulation and sampling to convert kinematic behaviors into a mathematical format, the system can perform efficient distance-metric searches across a database of effects. This allows the system to treat "kinematic behavior" as an searchable parameter, enabling users to transform and adapt existing particle assets (e.g., rotating or speeding up a trail to create a completely different effect) without needing to manually tune parameters.

    Potential Applications

  • **Game Development:** Allowing indie developers or novice level designers to quickly prototype and customize game environment effects (e.g., explosions, magic spells, environmental weather).
  • **Stylized Animation:** Assisting animators in generating complex visual effects without requiring deep expertise in simulation software.
  • **Digital Content Creation:** Serving as an accessible tool for creators who need to compose particle artworks by curating and modifying existing assets rather than building them from scratch.

    • Interesting Points

  • **Iterative Preference-Guided Search:** The system learns from the user's choices in each round, allowing the user to refine results by simply selecting the "most satisfying" candidate.
  • **Kinematic Transformation:** The system demonstrates that existing particle effects can be repurposed for new tasks through kinematic manipulation—such as turning a standard trail into an "acceleration effect" through speed and rotation changes.
  • **Semantic vs. Kinematic Balance:** Users have explicit control over the "emphasis" slider, allowing them to dictate how much the system prioritizes the *visual appearance* versus the *motion behavior* during the search process.
  • **Abstracted Control:** The system abstracts complex 3D dynamics into an intuitive representation, successfully hiding the underlying mathematical complexity from the user.

    • Limitations

  • **Scope of Particle Types:** The current system is primarily focused on game-style particle effects; the presenter noted that the current implementation is based on spherical coordinate systems, meaning it may not support all types of complex physical simulations (e.g., advanced mesh-based deformations or complex wind-based physics).
